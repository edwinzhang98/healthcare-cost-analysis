# Memo: Medicare readmission penalties and the proposed follow-up program

**To:** VP of Quality, CFO · **From:** Quality Analytics · **Date:** November 2026

## Bottom line

- **Do not fund a follow-up program for all eight hospitals.** It would cost about $1.1 million a year and win back only about $0.3 million in Medicare penalties.
- **Medicare's readmission penalty cost us an estimated $1.2 million in the 2026 payment year; two hospitals carried 62% of it:** Princeton (Plainsboro), mostly hip and knee replacement, and Inspira Mullica Hill, mostly heart failure. This year's cuts, already set, total about $0.4 million.
- **If we want fewer readmissions as a quality goal, start small:** a two-year pilot at Inspira Mullica Hill and Palisades, where heart failure is worst. It costs about $90,000 in year one ($130,000 a year at full size) and still loses about $33,000 a year at full effect; penalty relief starts only in late 2028. At the expected effect and cost, it pays for itself only if payers, Medicare included, return about 27% of their savings, so sign those terms first.

## Why

1. **Each prevented readmission costs more than it returns.** Even at the two pilot hospitals, preventing one costs about $18,000 in program spending against about $15,000 of penalty relief (group-wide: about $27,000 against $7,000), and the $17,000 saved on the avoided stay goes to the insurer, not to us.
2. **Our gaps are specific.** Seven of our eight hospitals readmit more than similar hospitals; five are in the bottom 20%. Heart failure at Inspira Mullica Hill, Palisades and Jersey City is worse than at 90% of similar hospitals. Good follow-up programs prevent about 1 in 10 readmissions; phone calls alone show no measurable effect.
3. **Penalties move slowly and swing on their own.** Medicare looks back two years, so a program starting in January 2027 first lowers our penalty in late 2028 and reaches its full effect only in late 2031. This year's cuts fell at five of our hospitals, including the two largest, held steady at one, and rose at Palisades and Jersey City.

## Recommendations

| Who | What | Expected result | How we'll know | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VP of Quality, CFO | No group-wide program this year | Avoid about $1.1 million a year of spending | Revisit after the pilot | Fewer patients helped now |
| Nursing leaders, Inspira Mullica Hill and Palisades | Pilot: medication review before discharge, nurse contact within 72 hours, visit within 7 days | About 7 fewer readmissions a year | First: confirm both sites in Medicare's hospital reports. Month 12: 90+ enrolled, 8 in 10 get all three steps, $400 or less each, no rise in ER visits, deaths or readmissions vs a comparison group. Month 24: keep only if enrolled patients' readmissions fall about 16% (12% with insurer co-funding) | Smaller effect than in studies; 300 patients cannot prove a 16% change alone, so read it with published trials |
| CFO, contracting | Ask the insurers we contract with to share their savings | Pilot breaks even at a 27% share | Terms signed before launch | Insurers may decline; Medicare shares savings only through its own programs |
| Orthopedics chair | Review recent hip and knee readmissions at Princeton | Hip and knee drove about $250,000 of our 2026 penalty estimate | Quarterly review | Problem may already be easing |
| Quality analytics | Track readmissions monthly; update with Medicare's 2027 data | Early warning | Monthly report | Rule changes |
