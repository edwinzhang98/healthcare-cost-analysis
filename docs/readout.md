# Memo: Medicare readmission penalties and the proposed follow-up program

**To:** VP of Quality, CFO · **From:** Quality Analytics · **Date:** November 2026

## Bottom line

- **Do not fund a follow-up program for all eight hospitals.** It would cost about $1.1 million a year and win back only about $0.3 million in Medicare penalties.
- **We lose about $1.2 million a year to Medicare's readmission penalty; two hospitals carry 62% of it:** Princeton (Plainsboro), mostly hip and knee replacement, and Inspira Mullica Hill, mostly heart failure.
- **If we want fewer readmissions as a quality goal, start small:** a 12-month pilot at Inspira Mullica Hill and Palisades, where heart failure is worst. It costs about $130,000 a year and still loses about $33,000 a year even at full effect; penalty relief only starts in late 2028. It pays for itself only if insurers return about 27% of their savings, so sign those terms first.

## Why

1. **Each prevented readmission costs more than it returns.** Preventing one costs about $18,000 in program spending. Medicare's penalty relief for it is about $15,000, and the $17,000 saved on the avoided stay goes to the insurer, not to us.
2. **Our gaps are specific.** Seven of our eight hospitals readmit more patients than similar hospitals; five are in the bottom 20%. Heart failure at Inspira Mullica Hill, Palisades and Jersey City is worse than at 90% of similar hospitals. Good follow-up programs prevent about 1 in 10 readmissions; phone calls alone show no measurable effect.
3. **Penalties move slowly and swing on their own.** Medicare looks back two years, so a program starting in January 2027 first lowers our penalty in late 2028 and reaches its full effect only in late 2031. This year's penalties fell at five of our hospitals but rose at Palisades and Jersey City.

## Recommendations

| Who | What | Expected result | How we'll know | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VP of Quality, CFO | No group-wide program this year | Avoid about $1.1 million a year of spending | Revisit after the pilot | Fewer patients helped now |
| Nursing leaders, Inspira Mullica Hill and Palisades | Pilot: medication review before discharge, nurse contact within 72 hours, visit within 7 days | About 7 fewer readmissions a year | Month 12: patients get all three steps at $400 or less each. | Orthopedics chair | Review recent hip and knee readmissions at Princeton | Hip and knee drove about $250,000 of the 2026 penalty | Quarterly review | Problem may already be easing |
| Quality analytics | Track readmissions monthly; update with Medicare's 2027 data | Early warning | Monthly report | Rule changes |
